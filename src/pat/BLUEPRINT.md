# School-paper pattern (learned from the user's real school papers, 2026-27)

## Why our earlier papers differed
* Earlier: 40 questions = 20 MCQ + 8x2 + 8x3 + 4x5, no case studies, no Assertion-Reason, text-only, recall/formula level, chapter-balanced.
* Real school papers (CBSE-style, 80 marks, 3 h):
  - MATHS (Class 9 and 10): 38 questions. Sec A 20 x 1 (Q1-18 MCQ incl. figure/data-based, Q19-20 Assertion-Reason); Sec B 5 x 2 (internal choice in 2 questions); Sec C 6 x 3 (choice in 2); Sec D 4 x 5 (choice in 2); Sec E 3 case-study questions x 4 marks, sub-parts (1)+(1)+(2), choice only in the 2-mark sub-part. 20+10+18+20+12 = 80.
  - SCIENCE (Class 9 and 10): 39 questions. Sec A 20 x 1 (16 MCQ + 4 Assertion-Reason; many MCQs are scenario/application based; some figure based); Sec B 6 x 2 (choice in 2); Sec C 7 x 3 (choice in 2); Sec D 3 x 5 (choice in all/2-3); Sec E 3 case-study x 4 (1+1+2, choice in the 2-mark part). 20+12+21+15+12 = 80. Class 9 school papers may instead be sectioned by subject (Biology / Chemistry / Physics) with the same mixture of types inside each; use ONE consistent design: sections A-E as above, with questions ordered Bio -> Chem -> Phys inside each section and balanced across the three disciplines (approx. Bio 27, Chem 26, Phy 27 marks).
* Style of the real questions (must be imitated):
  - ~50% competency / application: a short real-life scenario with Indian names/places/objects (a farmer trimming his garden, sugarcane chewing, mango sapling in monsoon winds, glass factory, aerospace engineer, telecom recharge plan, tyre company records, circus tent, school fitness challenge, carpet design with quadrants, locker code, picnic coordinates, Maglev vs slow train, cricket fielder catching a ball) followed by 2-3 sub-questions that need reasoning, not recall.
  - MCQs ask "which of the following is INCORRECT / correctly matches", statement-based, "(I)(II)(III)(IV) options a. (I) and (II)...", predict-the-outcome, with plausible distractors; numeric MCQs need 1-2 steps.
  - Case studies: 40-80 word story + (often) a figure + 3 parts (1, 1, 2 marks); mixture of recall, calculation and 'explain why'. The OR in a case study is on the 2-mark part.
  - Short/long answers: NCERT-type but mixed with application: derive/prove (angle at centre is double; BPT; sqrt2 irrational; second equation of motion), constructions (triangle + circumcircle), graph drawing, grouped-data mean with missing frequency, 3-coin probability, distinguish-between, numericals with units, labelled diagrams (biology), 'identify and write one function' with figure.
  - Figures are very common: coordinate plots, circles with chords/tangents/angles, cyclic quadrilaterals, shaded regions, solids (tent = cylinder+cone), force diagrams, tissue/cell diagrams, number lines. Provide a real SVG figure (see figs.py; write extra raw SVG as needed) wherever a school paper would show one. Every figure must be geometrically consistent with the question numbers.
  - Numbers are clean (answers neat); units always stated; 'Use pi = 22/7 / 3.14' stated.
  - Internal-choice alternatives are of EQUAL difficulty and cover the same unit (OR = parallel question).
  - Do NOT copy any question from the school papers; write fresh original questions in the same style. (Topics in them overlap NCERT; that is fine.)
* Difficulty mix per paper (CBSE competency): ~50% competency-based, 20% select-response/figure, 30% constructed response. Roughly 30% easy, 50% average, 20% difficult.
* Coverage: full syllabus every set (term-end style), different chapter emphasis per set; across 5 sets all chapters repeat but with different questions; no duplicate question across sets.
